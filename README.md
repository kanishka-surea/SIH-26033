## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Expected Solution: Create a digital marketplace that:

• Connects farmers/FPOs directly with consumers and bulk buyers.
• Provides logistics support.
• Uses AI for demand forecasting and route optimization.

Benefits:

• Better prices for farmers.
• Lower prices for consumers.
• Reduced supply chain inefficiencies.

## Proposed Solution
Right now, a farmer grows the vegetables, but a chain of agents and traders decides how much he actually earns. FarmDirect changes that. Farmers can sell through a village selling center, a simple phone call, or a mobile app, whichever feels comfortable to them. Buyers get the produce straight from the source, and the money goes straight to the farmer.
Every middleman takes a cut, and that cut is paid by the farmer in lower earnings and by the consumer in higher prices. By removing those extra layers, both sides win. Farmers keep more of what their crop is worth, families pay less at the market, and everyone can see where the money goes.
Most digital solutions assume everyone has a smartphone. We don't. A farmer who is not comfortable with technology can call a helpline and speak in his own language, or walk into a nearby center and sell face to face. Our idea fits the farmer, not the other way around.
## Technical Approach
We'll build a simple website and mobile app using React and Flutter, with Node.js and a database like MongoDB behind them. For farmers without smartphones, a voice-based helpline (IVR) will work in regional languages. Payments will go through UPI or bank transfer, and each village center will need just a computer or tablet and a digital weighing scale.
A farmer registers by calling, visiting a center, or using the app, and tells us what he has and how much. He brings the produce to the nearest center, where it is weighed and checked in front of him. Buyers place orders, and the produce is delivered or picked up. Once the sale is done, the payment goes directly to the farmer

## Feasibility and Viability
The idea doesn't need expensive or new technology. Mobile phones and UPI are already part of everyday life in many villages. We can start small with a pilot in one district, learn from it, and grow step by step.
There are challenges. Not every farmer is comfortable with technology, vegetables spoil quickly if delayed, existing middlemen may resist the change, and running centers and a helpline costs money. We'll handle this by providing helpline support and trained local people at each center, using fast collection and nearby delivery to keep produce fresh, working with farmer groups to build trust, and covering costs with a small service fee of around 3-5%, much less than what middlemen take.
## Impact and Benefits
For farmers, this means better income, timely payment, and more respect for their hard work. For consumers, it means fresher vegetables at fairer prices.
Socially, farmers are no longer exploited and local people get jobs at the centers. Economically, farmers earn more, consumers spend less, and money stays in the local economy. Environmentally, less food is wasted and shorter travel distances mean less fuel burned.
